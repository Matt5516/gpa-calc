How to calculate your GPA

Short answer: turn each grade into points, multiply by the credits, add it all up, and divide by the total credits. Four classes of 3, 4, 3 and 2 credits with an A, B+, A− and C+ come to 3.41.

The formula

Quality points = grade points × credits GPA = total quality points ÷ total credits

Worked example

CourseGradePoints × creditsQuality points
Course 1A4.0 × 312.0
Course 2B+3.3 × 413.2
Course 3A−3.7 × 311.1
Course 4C+2.3 × 24.6
Total12 credits40.9

40.9 ÷ 12 = 3.408, so your GPA is 3.41, about a B+ average. Skip the credits and average the grade points alone and you get 3.325: that is the common mistake, because a 4-credit class counts more than a 2-credit one.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my GPA?

Multiply each grade’s points by its credits, add those quality points, and divide by the total credits. An A (4.0) in 3 credits, a B+ (3.3) in 4, an A− (3.7) in 3 and a C+ (2.3) in 2 give 40.9 quality points over 12 credits: 40.9 ÷ 12 = 3.41.

How do I calculate my cumulative GPA?

Add this term’s quality points to the quality points you already have (GPA × credits so far) and divide by all the credits. A 3.2 GPA over 45 credits plus a 40.9-point, 12-credit term gives (144.0 + 40.9) ÷ 57 = 3.24.

What GPA do I need next term to reach my target?

Needed average = (target × total credits − current GPA × credits so far) ÷ credits to come. From a 3.2 over 60 credits, a 3.34 target and 30 more credits you need a 3.62 average.

What do I need on my final?

Needed score = (target − grade now × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight. With an 88% now, a final worth 25% and a target of 87%, you need 84%.

What’s the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?

Unweighted GPA uses plain grade points on a 4.0 scale. Weighted GPA adds extra points for honors, AP, IB or dual enrollment classes, so it can go above 4.0. The bumps differ by school.

Can I change the grading scale?

Yes. Every tool has a Scale section where you can pick a preset (standard 4.0 with +/−, 4.0 without, or 4.3) or edit any letter’s points and percent cutoff. Your edits stay in the share link.

Does TermMath store my grades?

No. Your grades stay in your browser. They only show up in the page link if you share it.
