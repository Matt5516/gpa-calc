Final grade calculator

What do you need on the final? Here it is.

The math: Needed score = (target − grade now × (1 − weight)) ÷ weight. Your grade now covers the rest of the course, so it counts for (1 − weight). After the final: course grade = grade now × (1 − weight) + final score × weight.

The short answer

With an 88% now and a final worth 25%, a B+ (87%) needs an 84% on the final: (87 − 88 × 0.75) ÷ 0.25 = 84. Ace it and you finish with 91.0%; skip it and you finish with 66.0%.

When the target is out of reach

With an 84% now and a final worth 30%, a 90% needs a 104% on the final. That is not possible without extra credit. The best you can finish with is 88.8%, a B+.

When you have already locked it in

With a 95% now and a final worth 20%, a 70% needs −30%, which is below zero. You have already locked in a C no matter what you score on the final.

Targets can be a letter (A, B+) or a percent. A plain A, B, C or D means any grade of that letter, so B is 80% on the default scale. Your syllabus may use different cutoffs, and some teachers round up or curve. Ask yours.

FAQ

How do I calculate what I need on my final exam?

Needed score = (target − grade now × (1 − final weight)) ÷ final weight. For an 88 now, a final worth 25% and a target of 87: (87 − 88 × 0.75) ÷ 0.25 = 84%.

What if I need more than 100%?

Then the target is out of reach without extra credit. With an 84% now and a 30% final, the best you can finish with is 88.8%, so a 90% is not possible.

What do I need on the final to get an A, B or C?

The calculator lists all four letters: an A (90%), B (80%), C (70%) and D (60%), with the score you need for each at your grade and final weight.

How much is my final worth?

The weight is in your syllabus: often 10% to 40%. If it is worth 100% of the grade, enter 100.

How do I find my course grade after the final?

Use After the final: grade now × (1 − weight) + final score × weight. With 88, 25% and a 72 on the final, that is 84.0%.

Does it round up?

The needed score is rounded up to the next tenth so that hitting it is enough. Your teacher’s rounding or curve may help you beyond that.
