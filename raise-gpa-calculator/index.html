The short answer

To lift a 3.2 GPA over 60 credits to a 3.34 with 30 more credits, you need a 3.62 average over those 30 credits: (3.34 × 90 − 192.0) ÷ 30 = 3.62.

The more credits you have, the slower your GPA moves. Each new credit is a smaller share of the total. That is why raising a GPA gets harder every term, and why the first steps count most.

When a target is out of reach

If the average you need is above the top of your scale, the planner says so, shows the highest GPA you can reach in those credits, and tells you how many credits of straight A’s it takes. For a gentler plan, aim for a nearer target first. If you are already above your target, it shows the lowest average that keeps you there.

Schools set their own scales. Check yours and edit the scale if it’s different: open Scale under the form and change any letter’s points or percent cutoff. Your edits stay in the share link.

FAQ

How do I calculate the GPA I need?

Needed average = (target × total credits − current GPA × credits so far) ÷ credits to come. For 3.2 over 60 credits, target 3.34, 30 more credits: (3.34 × 90 − 192) ÷ 30 = 3.62.

How many credits does it take to raise my GPA?

It depends on the average you earn. From 3.2 over 60 credits, reaching 3.34 takes about 12.7 credits of straight A’s, 23.3 credits at a 3.7 average or 52.5 credits at a 3.5.

What if the target is impossible?

If you have 2.5 over 90 credits and want a 3.0 in 15 credits, you would need a 6.0, which is above 4.0. The best you can reach is 2.71. The planner shows that and the credits of A’s that would do it.

Does retaking a class change the plan?

It can. If your school replaces the old grade, the old quality points leave your total. See the cumulative GPA calculator’s retake option.

Is the 4.0 limit editable?

The top of the scale comes from your grading scale. Open Scale to change it.
